# Savaya Capital — Deck V1 Analysis & Build-Out to 15–16 Pages

*Confidential Relationship Memorandum · issued July 2026 · currently 12 pages*

---

## 1. First, get clear on what this deck is FOR

The website and the deck are two different instruments and should almost **never repeat each other**.

| | **Website (savayacapital.com)** | **This deck (Relationship Memorandum)** |
|---|---|---|
| Audience | The public, founders, the curious | A *named* peer principal / co-investor / family office |
| Job | Signal who Savaya is; be discreet | Prove it, with numbers and specifics; earn reciprocity |
| Register | Philosophy, values, no figures | Evidence, mechanics, terms |
| Test for content | "Would we say this to anyone?" | "Would we only say this to someone we've decided to trust?" |

The deck's reader already knows the *philosophy* from the site (or a conversation). They open this to answer four private questions:

1. **Is the capital real, and really permanent?**
2. **Is this run seriously — governed, structured, audited?**
3. **Is the track record real — with attribution and marks?**
4. **Who am I actually dealing with, and exactly what are they proposing?**

Everything that only the deck can carry is an answer to one of those four. That's your filter.

## 2. Honest read of the current 12 pages

**What's already excellent (keep, these are pure deck-only value):**

- **P2 Capital base** — the $40M, 75/25, "no LPs, no capital calls, no redemption." This is exactly what the site withholds. Real and specific.
- **P5 Governance & decision rights** — voting thresholds, the reputational veto "above the vote." This is the single most credibility-building page in the deck. Peer principals love this.
- **P8 Structure & jurisdiction** — ADGM holdco / Cayman sub. Signals real institutional plumbing.
- **P7 Valuation discipline** — "marked conservatively on purpose." Directly answers "can I trust your numbers." Excellent.
- **P10 Why partner now / The Ask** — deployment window, cheque size, the explicit reciprocity ask. This is the *point* of the whole document.

**What's redundant with the website (differentiate or it wastes a page):**

- **P3 Investment process** — nearly identical to the About page's four stages. In a confidential memo this should go *deeper* than the site: add real timelines (how long each stage takes), who does what, a real example. Otherwise it's filler the reader has already seen.
- **P4 Sector thesis** — overlaps the site's five sectors. The deck version should show *what we actually own / where we're positioned* in each theme, not restate the thesis. Right now it's a sharper restatement, not new information.

**What's thin or unfinished (the credibility risk):**

- **P9 Track record** — this is the most important page and it's full of placeholders: `[$ / terms]`, `[Realized position]`, `[MOIC / IRR]`, `[Role]`. A memorandum with brackets reads as unfinished and quietly undermines everything else. This must be filled with real (even if rounded/redacted) figures, including **at least one realized position and one honest write-down** — showing a loss you handled well builds more trust than a flawless sheet.
- **P10** still has `[region]` and `[date]` placeholders.

**The single biggest gap: there are no people in this deck.**
A family office is a *trust* business, and the reader is deciding whether to deal with *you specifically*. There is currently no principals page, no CIO, no bios, no "who sits on that IC." You reference "two Family Directors and a CIO" on P5 but never say who they are or what they've done. For a peer-to-peer relationship memo, that's the most conspicuous omission.

## 3. The deck-only information that should carry the weight

These are the things a counterparty *needs* and can *only* get here — grouped by the four questions. Add the missing ones; they're what take you to 15–16 pages with substance rather than padding.

**Q1 — Is the capital real and permanent?** *(mostly covered)*
- ✅ P2 capital base. Consider adding: **source of capital** (one line on where the wealth comes from — operating business, liquidity event — as much as you'll disclose), and **liquidity posture** (how much is callable in days vs locked), which reassures a co-investor you can actually fund your side.

**Q2 — Is this run seriously?** *(strong, one addition)*
- ✅ Governance (P5), structure (P8), valuation (P7).
- **ADD — Risk & liquidity management:** position limits, max single-name concentration, currency/counterparty policy, how the credit sleeve is used as "dry optionality." This is deck-only by definition and peer principals will look for it.
- **ADD — Service providers / institutional stack:** auditor, fund admin, legal counsel, banking/custody relationships. Naming real institutions is fast, powerful proof you're not a hobby.

**Q3 — Is the track record real?** *(needs finishing + depth)*
- **FIX P9** with real attribution: entry basis, current mark, realized vs unrealized, role (board/observer/passive), and honest MOIC/IRR where realized. Include a write-down.
- **ADD — One or two position deep-dives (case studies):** pick your best story (e.g. the OpenAI secondary, or SF Compute where you hold a board seat) and show *how you sourced it, why you underwrote it, what you did after.* A single credible case study is worth more than the whole logo wall — and it's inherently confidential.

**Q4 — Who am I dealing with, and what's proposed?** *(biggest opportunity)*
- **ADD — Principals & team page:** the two Family Directors and the CIO — names or discreet initials, backgrounds, what each owns in the process. Even anonymized ("Principal, 20 yrs in X") this changes the document.
- **ADD — Origination edge / proprietary network:** *why you see deals others don't.* The site claims "proprietary networks"; the deck should prove it — sourcing channels, operator relationships, how a real deal actually came in the door. This is what a reciprocity partner is buying.
- **ADD — How co-investment actually works with you:** the economics and mechanics. As an SFO you presumably charge no fees / carry — **say so explicitly**, it's a huge differentiator vs a fund. Typical structure (SPV, direct, syndicate), decision speed, what you offer a partner (capital + operating help + board coverage), what you expect back.
- ✅ P10 The Ask + P11 next steps already close this well.

**Optional but valuable additions:**
- **Alignment page:** "every dollar we deploy is our own family's — we sit in the same security as our partners, at the same basis." One page, enormous trust value, and impossible on a public site.
- **Time horizon / what 'permanent' means in practice:** an illustrative hold-period vs a typical fund's 7–10yr clock. Reinforces the core differentiator with a visual.

## 5. Flags before this goes to anyone

- **Kill every `[bracket]` placeholder.** A confidential memorandum with `[region]`, `[date]`, `[$ / terms]`, `[MOIC / IRR]` visible reads as a draft and undercuts the institutional tone. Fill or remove.
- **Decide the disclosure line on people and numbers.** You don't have to name the family; you *do* have to give the reader enough to trust a person, not just a brand. Pick the discreet-but-real middle.
- **Keep the deck's numbers consistent with the site's posture** — the site never states AUM publicly (correct); the deck states $40M (correct). Just make sure any figure that ever appears in both matches.
- **One typo to fix:** P6 header reads "DELIGENCE" — should be "DILIGENCE." (Same misspelling once appeared on the site; worth a global check.)
- **The "$40M / cheque $3–8M / deploy $15–25M over 12–24 months" math should reconcile.** $15–25M deployment against a $40M base with ~$10M liquid-now implies recycling/realizations funding the rest — make that logic explicit so a sharp reader doesn't catch a gap.
